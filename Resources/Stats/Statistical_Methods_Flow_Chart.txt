
Hypothesis tests are chosen based on the data type (continuous vs. categorical), number of groups, sample size/distribution assumptions (parametric vs. non-parametric), and study design (independent vs. dependent).

Decision guide to help select the correct statistical test based on your data type, group count, and design.

---

## Decision Guide: How to Choose the Right Statistical Test

### Step 1: Identify Your Primary Goal & Data Type

         ▼                                                       ▼

---

### Section A: Categorical Data (Counts, Proportions, Binary)

* **1 Sample vs. Expected Distribution:**
* **Chi-Squared Goodness-of-Fit Test** — e.g., *Is a 6-sided die fair?*


* **1 Group vs. Known Proportion:**
* **One-Sample Z-Test for Proportions** — e.g., *Is the coin flip heads rate significantly different from 50%?*


* **2 Independent Groups:**
* **Two-Sample Z-Test for Proportions** — e.g., *A/B testing conversion rates on two different landing pages.*


* **2 Paired / Matched Groups:**
* **McNemar’s Test** — e.g., *Yes/No voting preference before and after watching a debate.*


* **2+ Categorical Variables (Relationship / Independence):**
* **Chi-Squared Test of Independence** — e.g., *Is smoking status (Yes/No) independent of age group?*
* *(If sample sizes are very small, use **Fisher’s Exact Test** instead).*

## Quick Reference Summary Table

| What are you testing? | Normal / Parametric Test | Non-Parametric Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 Mean vs. Known Value** | One-Sample $t$-test | Wilcoxon Signed-Rank (1-sample) |
| **2 Independent Means** | Independent Samples $t$-test | Mann-Whitney U Test |
| **2 Paired / Matched Means** | Paired Samples $t$-test | Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test |
| **3+ Independent Means** | One-Way ANOVA | Kruskal-Wallis Test |
| **3+ Repeated Measurements** | Repeated Measures ANOVA | Friedman Test |
| **Relationship between 2 variables** | Pearson Correlation | Spearman Rank Correlation |

---

### Key Checkpoints Before Running Parametric Tests:

1. **Normality:** Check if your continuous data is normally distributed (use **Shapiro-Wilk Test**). If violated, switch to the Non-Parametric alternative.
2. **Equal Variances:** For independent $t$-tests or ANOVA, check if variances across groups are equal (use **Levene’s Test**). If violated, use **Welch’s $t$-test** or **Welch's ANOVA**.
